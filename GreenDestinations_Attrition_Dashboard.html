Key Insights

1. Age is a major driver: Employees under 25 have the highest attrition rate (39.2%), followed by the 25–34 band (20.2%). Younger staff are significantly more likely to leave.

2. Low income correlates with leaving: Employees who left earned on average $4,787/month — $2,046 less than those who stayed ($6,833). Salary competitiveness is critical.

3. Early tenure is a danger zone: 29.8% of employees with 2 or fewer years at the company left — nearly 3x the rate of those with 6–10 years. Onboarding and early engagement matter most.

4. Overtime triples attrition risk: Overtime workers leave at 30.5% vs 10.4% for those without overtime — a 3x multiplier. Workload management is a clear lever.

5. Sales roles are highest risk: Sales Representatives have a 39.8% attrition rate, nearly double the company average. Department-level retention strategies are needed.

About This Project

What is it?

This is an HR Attrition Analysis Dashboard for Green Destinations, a well-known travel agency. It analyses data collected from 1,470 employees to identify why staff are leaving the company and what patterns exist across departments, age groups, income levels, and working habits.

Why was it made?

The HR Director noticed a rise in employee departures and needed a clear, data-driven picture of the problem. Instead of guessing, the team surveyed all staff and built this dashboard to pinpoint the biggest risk factors — so leadership could act on facts, not feelings.

How was it built?

The raw employee survey data (CSV) was analysed to calculate attrition rates, income gaps, and tenure patterns across every segment. The results were then visualised as an interactive dashboard using Chart.js, with the Green Destinations branding applied throughout — built with IBM Bob.
